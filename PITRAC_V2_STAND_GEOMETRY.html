Next Gen PiTrac — Stand Geometry: How and Why It Was Chosen

Where the two Mira220 cameras, the IR strobe, the light-sheet trigger, the V2 board and its microphone sit relative to the ball, how a two-month optimization campaign arrived at that layout, how closely the built stand matches it, and what it implies for the firmware. Part I

The Geometry

1.1 Frames & Conventions

All coordinates use the RH ball frame, the frame the optimizer's last CAD output (reference_003) and the printed stand parts were drawn in: origin on the floor directly under the ball, +X downrange along the target line, +Y from the stand towards the ball, +Z up. The stand stands on the floor beside a hitting mat assumed to be 36 mm thick, so the mat top is Z = 36 and a resting ball's centre is Z = 57.3.

One stand, both hands

For left-handed play the same stand is turned 180° about Z and placed at Y = +408.1 instead of −408.1. Cameras, focus, strobe and trigger never move relative to each other.

Other frames you will meet

Optimizer world: as RH, but Z = 0 at the mat top (RH Z = world Z + 36). Chassis: the stand's own frame; RH = chassis + (65, −408.1, 33.5). SolidWorks assembly: RH = assembly − (597.67, 1296.56, 1251.95).

The 36 mm assumption

If the stand stands on the mat instead of the floor beside it, every height relative to the ball is 36 mm too high. Nothing has measured the real mat.

Part II

Why This Layout

2.1 The Requirements It Was Optimized Against

The owner confirmed these in the optimizer project's 5 and 6 September 2026 handoffs.

One rigid unit, both hands

To switch hands, move the intact unit to the marked handed position. No recalibration, no refocusing. The cameras and strobe never move when the trigger is toggled.

The shots

requireddrivers, irons, wedges and chips, both hands. wantedlob/flop. Putting: at least one camera must see the ball, under different lighting.

The hitting area

A shared 150 × 100 mm area strongly desired, not required. Different recommended ball positions for driver, irons and short game are allowed.

The envelope

Finished enclosure ≤ 350 mm wide, ≤ 350 mm high, face ≤ 70,000 mm². No larger fallback.

The trigger and strobe model

Hardware-triggered Mira220s; speed from the time the ball takes to cross one light sheet, modelled 30 mm thick; exposure and strobing start after the trailing edge, 25 µs readiness assumed; a fixed fallback burst when speed is unusable.

How the fixed spine became the baseline
No formal ranking in the records selects it outright. The fixed spine met the "one rigid unit, no moving parts" requirement with the widest validated zone, and on 9 September the owner asked for the workbench's default, vertical spine, fixed trigger, level CAM0, to be the baseline of the next search. Its lob/flop weakness was then attacked by moving the upper camera and strobe, not by changing family: weighted020 reaches 69 % mean lob/flop capture in its chosen zone.

2.5 The Searches

Compact-250 (Sep 9–10)

Every component datum ≤ 250 mm. 20 variables: Y, Z and pitch of each of the four components; X, Y and yaw of each handed dock; both focus distances. Component X fixed, positions shared by both hands. 1,672 trials. The best (Candidate B) beat the 95.64 % baseline on every category but scored slightly worse on placement robustness.

Region 150 × 100 (Sep 14)

Height cap raised to 300 mm. Objective: the best movable 150 × 100 mm rectangle per hand, 90 % its quality (half mean, half weakest-10 % mean) and 10 % the area outside it, ranked by the worse hand.

Weighted per-club (Sep 15)

No new search: all 341 saved capture maps (336 unique, 335 within the 300 mm cap) rescored with the objective in §2.6. Trial 20 of the region search won.

2.8 From Reference to Built Stand

Kept from the design

Camera pupils, axes and focus distances; strobe height and pitch; the trigger anchor's position; the handed dock pose; the 36 mm floor convention. The printed parts were drawn directly in reference_003's RH frame.

Changed

The V2 board became the trigger. D11 shines through Lens_LED and D12 looks through Lens_Photodiode, with D11 placed exactly at the old trigger's height on the X = 65 plane. The optimizer modelled the earlier separate trigger and an ideal 30 mm slab.

Added, never modelled

The Pi 5 behind the board, an LRS-75 supply on the base, a switch near its foot. None was in the optimizer's obstruction model, and the Oct 1 keep-clear screen ran on the old trigger's lenses.

3.2 What the Score Hides

largestFocus sensitivity

A ±10 µm image-plane error in either camera drops the weighted score from 97.20 % to 79.30 %. Focus repeatability on real lenses is unmeasured.

weakLob/flop

69 % mean, 50 % lowest sample, hidden by its 3 % weight.

idealizedOptics

Pinhole projection, no measured distortion, a single hand-measured pupil offset, no exact lens body.

assumedTiming

25 µs readiness and a 30 mm sheet, neither measured. 7c.1 has since measured the camera's trigger delay at about 0.2 µs.

warningsMechanics

Scored with mechanics as warnings; a legacy projection warning on the strobe (9.09 mm) remains.

not accuracyWhat the % means

The fraction of a 333-shot catalog the model expects to capture usefully. None of this is measured ball speed, launch or spin accuracy.

3.3 Two Timing Models That Disagree

Where 150 mm came from

A deliberately loose starting value. §15 sized it for a camera delay assumed at 100–300 µs ("holds to ~90 m/s+"), and §16 step 8 says to tighten it from the images. Even with 300 µs the design's own formula needs only about 53 mm at 90 m/s. Neither optimizer used it.

How early the first freeze can come

The design formula's floor is (D + w)/2 + v · 32 µs past the sheet centre: 39.5 mm at 100 m/s. The live current readback sets a larger one: a live firing waits for 200 µs of ADC0 baseline after the switch to BURST, 198–200 µs on board 1. With 32 µs of detection, try about 230 µs of readiness below: four whole-ball freezes still fit at 100 m/s (7c.3, passed 2026-11-20). The shot path is planned in Hardware/firmware/SHOT_PATH_PLAN.md.

Why it matters

With 150 mm, only about 2 whole-ball freezes land in CAM0's view; with the optimizer's model, about 4. An earlier burst, though, gives the microphone less time (§3.4).
tFIRE(150 mm model) = (Xs + B) / v     tFIRE(optimizer) = (Xs + (D + w)/2) / v + tready     tsound = 454.6 mm / 343 m/s = 1.325 ms
Xs = 65 mm, D = 42.67 mm, w = sheet thickness, B = beam_to_fov. Times from first club contact, assuming the ball moves at full speed from X = 0; the ~0.45 ms of club contact actually delays it by roughly 0.2 ms, which moves every FIRE later.

4.2 Making a New Design

  1. Check the copy: run Verify_Optimizer.cmd (about 40 s; must say PASSED).
  2. Edit and score: Launch_Workbench.cmd, then http://127.0.0.1:8765. Pin weighted020 as the reference, edit poses, focus, docks, ball starts or timing, and run Evaluate, Focus check, Capture map and Active-LUT64.
  3. Search (optional): copy optimizer/work/d_optimization_v3/region150x100_20260914/campaign_v2.py into a new dated folder, change BOUNDS or the objective, then run its init and search phases with the bundled Python. The originals are templates: they check historical run folders that were not copied.
  4. Export: Export STEP in the workbench gives chassis and world STEPs, RH and LH, with camera FOV, strobe and sheet references. The world frame has the mat top at Z = 0; add the mat thickness to match the RH floor frame. Needs FreeCAD 1.1.3.
  5. Draw the parts in SolidWorks around the imported components, in that frame, as FullStand was.
  6. Check the build: python Hardware/Stand/tools/stand_geometry.py --step <assembly.STEP> in this repo, after replacing Hardware/Stand/reference_003/ with the new scene and datums.
Traps
The engine hashes every module it loads into a source identity, refuses to start if its historical coaxial sources differ from their snapshot or a pinned input fails its hash, and stops if a file changes mid-session. Never let line endings change in optimizer/. Builders and searches refuse an existing output folder: copy into a new dated folder, change the copy.

Status
