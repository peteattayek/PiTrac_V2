Part I

Next Gen PiTrac vs. Current PiTrac

Current PiTrac uses a passive, all-through-hole connector PCB with no onboard microcontroller, driven directly by Raspberry Pi 5 Linux userspace GPIO and SPI calls. Next Gen PiTrac replaces that board with a turnkey SMD real-time controller & connector board built around an onboard Raspberry Pi RP2354B (U3), onto which the Raspberry Pi 5 mounts directly via 40-pin socket J8.

Core Design Split
"The RP2354 owns everything with microsecond timing. The Pi owns everything with pixels." Inside the Next Gen firmware, "the CPU orchestrates, hardware executes": carrier/demod clocks, ball-transit timing, dual-camera shutter handshaking, strobe pulse trains, and continuous ADC ring sampling run on dedicated PWM/PIO/DMA hardware state machines with zero OS or ISR jitter.

1.2 Nine Core Improvements Explained

1 · PIO + DMA Deterministic Sub-μs Triggering

Current PiTrac polls 232 fps Camera 1 frames in Linux OpenCV (ball moves halfway across FOV before strobing) and shifts 800 KB SPI buffers whose rate drifts with Pi CPU clocks unless force_turbo=1 is set. Next Gen PiTrac runs transit timing on PIO2, camera sync on PIO1, and strobe bursts on PIO0 with cross-block irq prev set 4 handoff and ~31 μs analog latency.

2 · 104.17 kHz Modulated IR Light-Sheet Trigger

Replaces visible tee-lighting + video motion polling with a cylindrical IR light curtain (D11 @ ~3 A peak) and 36 V reverse-biased PIN photodiode D12 + 2.27 Hz DC servo + phase-locked synchronous demodulator (~48 dB 120 Hz rejection), measuring ball launch speed before capture.

3 · Acoustic Onboard Analog + Digital Mic

Adds an always-on onboard analog MEMS mic (CMM-2718AT + LMV321 2.4–24 kHz band-pass, 250 ksps on ADC7, 51–54 dB SNR) for exact club-strike timestamping and practice-swing veto, plus header J5 for an optional I²S or PDM digital mic.

4 · 9 A Cascode Strobe Power, Speed & Safety

4× boost recharge (0.4 A LM5157 vs. 0.1 A MC33063), 2× bulk capacitance (~660 μF + 8 MLCCs), pre-settled 10-bit linear sink Q9 + 12 V series switch Q10 (~20 ns rise), dynamic per-shot blur scheduling (w = 1 mm / v), 500 ksps ADC0 current verification, and U5 ~135 μs hardware clamp.

5 · Stereo Closed-Loop Dual-Camera Triggering

Single-Pi Current PiTrac ties up Cam 1 free-running and triggers only Cam 2 open-loop (requiring two Pi 5s for true stereo). Next Gen PiTrac puts both cameras in external-trigger mode on one Pi 5, asserting shared D_Cam_Trigger and waiting in PIO1 until both Cam_Strobe_0/1 lines confirm exposure open in the same clock cycle.

6 · Soft Latch Safe Pi 5 Power Button

Always-on +3V3 (~32 mA) powers the RP2354B while high-side P-FET Q3 holds +5V off. Button press boots the Pi (if +5V_IN ≥ 5.05 V) or pulses RPI5_SHUTDOWN (GPIO26) low for 200 ms, cutting +5V only after the Pi's shutdown hook verifies all block mounts are read-only (plus a 5 s force-off hold).

7 · No USB-C Direct Header Power & Brownout Guard

Seats the Pi 5 directly onto J8 and feeds 5.20 V through two ≤ 3 mΩ SI7137DP FETs (eliminating USB-C cable/connector drop), protected by 86 ms boost soft-start, 4.52 V boost UVLO load-shedding above the Pi's 4.2 V crash point, and calibrated ADC1 supply guards.

8 · Zero USB Framed UART & A/B Rollback OTA

After one-time USB partition/calibration setup, runtime control and MCU updates run over 921.6 kbaud CRC-16 UART (J8.8/10). pitrac-link update stages new firmware in 288 KB RAM, cleanly shuts down the Pi, flashes the inactive A/B partition under the RP2350 boot ROM's 16.7 s Try-Before-You-Buy watchdog, and auto-reboots the Pi.

9 · Ecosystem Mira220 Raw V4L2 & Open Licensing

Evaluates 1600×1400 NIR-enhanced ams OSRAM Mira220 & IMX296 over direct V4L2 / RP1 CFE raw capture with ChArUco distortion tools, ships 273 native C + cycle-accurate PIO host tests, and pairs CERN-OHL-S v2 hardware licensing with GPL-3.0-or-later firmware/software.

System Wiring Diagram & Netlist-Verified Pinout Guide

Non-Negotiable Wiring & Assembly Safety Rules
1. Wire and re-seat ONLY while completely unpowered (both 5.2 V PSU on J1 and USB-C on J6 unplugged; wait for 36 V VIR caps to discharge).
2. NEVER wire J4 directly to a Mira220 camera. Mira220 digital I/O is strict 1.8 V with zero 3.3 V tolerance (CR-09). Always route through the HiLetgo TXS0108E level converter.
3. Feed TXS0108E VB (3.3 V) from PCB J5.2 (+3.3VA), NEVER from Mira220 P1.2 (3V3). Leave camera 3V3 unconnected on both cameras so a powered MCU never back-feeds an unpowered camera.
4. One TXS0108E per camera, each with VA + OE from its own camera's P1.5 (1V8) (owner, 2026-11-20). A camera's 1V8 is up only while it streams, so a module fed by the other camera would drive an unpowered camera's pins. Never short two camera LDOs together and never put both cameras' 12 kΩ REQ EXP pull-downs on one A pin.
5. Leave the seated Raspberry Pi 5's own USB-C power port UNPLUGGED, and tape over SW2 (RUN reset) so an MCU reset cannot hard-cut Pi power.
6. J3.2 (VIR_RTN) and TP5 (Strobe_GND) are NOT ground — never tie them to GND or scope earth, and keep nothing conductive near D12 (36 V cathode).

2.5 Mira220 Cameras via HiLetgo TXS0108E Level Converter (J4 + J5.2)

TXS0108E Wiring Rules: One Module per Camera (owner, 2026-11-20)
• One module per camera, wired alike: on each, B1→A1 (REQ EXP, with B5/A5 bridged onto it) and A2→B2 (ILLUM TRIG). Camera 0's module takes J4.3/J4.7; camera 1's takes J4.4/J4.8. Each Mira220 has an onboard R2 = 12 kΩ pull-down on REQ EXP; giving each camera its own channel against TXS0108E's internal 4 kΩ pull-up yields a clean 1.35 V HIGH plateau (>1.26 V VIH(min)), whereas tying two cameras to a single A pin (6 kΩ) sags to 1.08 V and fails.
• VA + OE from each camera's own P1.5 (1V8): Never short the two cameras' P1.5 together (each is an independent TCR2EF18LM LDO output).
• Why a module per camera: On the Mira220 board (MIRA220CSP-Sheet1.SchDoc), 1V8 is gated by sequencer U4 (LM3880) from each CSI port's CAM_ENABLE line, and the driver powers a camera only while it streams (board 1, 7c.0). A module fed by the other camera would drive an unpowered camera's pins; with its own module, an unpowered camera sees Hi-Z.
Why VB (3.3 V) CANNOT Come from the Camera's 3V3 Pin (Separate 3.3 V Domains!)
The Raspberry Pi 5's 3V3 rail and the custom PCB's +3V3 rail are NOT connected together (only GND is shared):
• Custom PCB +3V3 / +3.3VA (J5.2) comes from onboard LDO U2 (NCP1117-3.3) off always-on +5V_IN (ahead of latch Q3), so the RP2354B and its J4 I/O pins are live at 3.3 V even in STANDBY or BENCH_RUNNING.
• Pi 5 3V3 (J8.1 +3V3_Pi5) & Camera P1.2 (3V3 over CSI) come from the Pi 5's PMIC off switched +5V (behind latch Q3). On J8.1, +3V3_Pi5 feeds only a 110 kΩ sense divider (R45/R44 into GPIO24) — it never joins PCB +3V3!
• Therefore, whenever the Pi/camera is off while the PCB is on, Camera 3V3 sits at 0 V while PCB J4 sits in a live 3.3 V domain. Tying TXS0108E VB to Camera 3V3 violates TI's VA ≤ VB power rule and allows a 3.3 V high on D_Cam_Trigger (R18 = 220 Ω, up to ~12 mA) to back-power the unpowered camera's VDD33 rail and LM3880 sequencer through the translator! Tying VB to PCB J5.2 (+3.3VA) matches each side of the translator to the exact power domain of the pins connected to it.

2.7 Interactive Pre-Power Verification Checklist

  • All power removed during wiring: Both 5.20 V PSU (J1) and USB-C (J6) unplugged; J2 bypass jumper is OPEN.
  • DMM short check passed: No continuity between J1.1 (+VDC) & J1.2 (GND), between J3.1 (VIR) & J3.2 (VIR_RTN), or between J3.2 (VIR_RTN) & GND.
  • Camera 3V3 isolated: Mira220 P1.2 (3V3) is unconnected on both cameras; TXS0108E VB comes strictly from PCB J5.2 (+3.3VA).
  • One TXS0108E per camera: each module's VA + OE connects only to its own camera's P1.5 (1V8); module #0 on J4.3/J4.7, module #1 on J4.4/J4.8, nothing joining J4.7 and J4.8.
  • Raspberry Pi 5 seating: Plugged onto all 40 pins of J8 (Pin 1 to square Pad 1), Pi's own USB-C power port is empty, and SW2 is taped over.
  • PSU verified off-board: Meanwell LRS-75-5 measured at 5.20 V ± 0.03 V on a DMM and bled to 0 V before connecting J1.
  • 1.8 V trigger dry check: Before landing each module's A1 onto its Mira220's P1.7, verified each A1 swings to ~1.8 V (never 3.3 V).

Light-Sheet Optical Trigger: Modulation, Demodulation & Speed Theory

To freeze a golf ball ~10 times inside a single global-shutter frame across shots ranging from a 2 m/s putt to a 90 m/s drive, Next Gen PiTrac must detect launch within microseconds and measure ball velocity before the ball leaves the shared stereo camera field of view — all while rejecting sunlight, simulator projectors, 100/120 Hz LED room-light flicker, static mat reflections, and emitter thermal drift.

3.2 Optical Geometry & End-to-End Analog Signal Chain

Emitter D11 (VSMA1085250X02, 850 nm) and reverse-biased PIN photodiode D12 (VBPW34FAS) sit side by side behind a cylindrical plano-convex lens, focusing strongly along the downrange flight axis x while fanning vertically into a thin coincident backscatter curtain. As a ball of diameter D_ball = 42.67 mm crosses sheet profile W(x) at speed v, reflected optical power traces the spatial convolution P_ball(t) = R_ball ∫ W(x) B(x − vt) dx — a smooth positive bump whose width scales as 1/v.

3.4 Synchronous (Lock-In) Demodulation & Trapezoidal Phase Theory

At 150 MHz system clock with TOP = 1439 (1440 counts, divider 1), carrier slice 7B and demodulator slice 11B run at f_c = 104,166.67 Hz (T_c = 9.600 μs, 1 tick = 6.67 ns = 0.25°). U13 (TMUX1219) + U12A switch gain between +1 and −1 about +2V5, multiplying the TIA signal by a 50% bipolar square wave m(t) ∈ {+1, −1} containing only odd harmonics:

m(t) = (4 / π) · [ sin(ωct) + (1/3) sin(3ωct) + (1/5) sin(5ωct) + … ]
• Ball return at fc mixes down to 0 Hz (baseband) and passes cleanly through the 15.39 kHz 4th-order LPF.
• 100/120 Hz room flicker has no fc carrier, so multiplying by m(t) up-converts it to 104.17 kHz ± 120 Hz where the LPF rejects it (~48 dB measured).

Rn = [ 4 D ΔVTIA / (n π) ] · sinc(n D)   for odd n   |   phase_ticks(fc) = τchain · fsys − (D / 2)(TOP + 1)
cal demod keeps the demodulator clock running while chopping carrier duty (0% ↔ 25%) at 20 Hz across 64 phase offsets — sitting cleanly above the 2.27 Hz servo and 0.241 Hz HPF, and below the 15.39 kHz LPF.

3.5 Envelope Recovery & the Gated Baseline HPF (TRACK vs. HOLD)

Static emitter-to-photodiode leakage and mat reflection are modulated at f_c, producing a steady DC offset at TP9 that drifts slowly as D11 warms up. A fast fixed HPF would erase slow putts (~23 ms wide); a multi-second fixed HPF would take too long to settle. Next Gen PiTrac solves both with analog switch U14 (TMUX1219, GPIO33):

GPIO33 = 0 TRACK Mode (Disarmed / Idle)

Connects R96 (2 MΩ) to GND behind C81 (330 nF), forming a τ = 0.66 s (f_c = 0.241 Hz) high-pass filter that continuously zeroes static carrier return and thermal drift, holding U12B output (ADC5) near 0.0 V.

GPIO33 = 1 HOLD Mode (Armed for Shot)

Switches U14 to an open throw so C81 freezes the pre-shot baseline charge. The signal path becomes DC-coupled relative to the armed instant, preserving full pulse width and amplitude even on a 2 m/s putt (leakage walk is only 2.3–11 mV/s ≈ microvolts per shot).

3.6 Ball Speed Estimation & Interactive Dual-Path Simulator

Given calibrated effective optical interaction length L_eff (the bump's 50 %-of-peak width: about 36–41 mm in a simple backscatter model for the ~30 mm sheet, shorter than the full-contact D_ball + w_beam ≈ 72.7 mm; calibrated per board, not assumed) and measured transit time Δt, ball speed is v = L_eff / Δt. A fixed comparator threshold V_th (U15 → PIO2 SM0) triggers in ~1 μs, but suffers amplitude bias: dimmer or scuffed balls cross V_th later going up and earlier coming down, shortening Δt_cmp and overestimating speed! During the 100–300 μs wait for camera shutters to open, firmware extracts the waveform from the 250 ksps ADC5 ring and interpolates the 50%-of-own-peak (FWHM) crossings — where any reflectance scale factor α cancels identically!
